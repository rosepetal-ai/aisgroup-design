Adapt language, dates and numbers without changing the brand system.

## Principles

- **Localise, do not translate.** Keep the message, the evidence and the call to action; rewrite the sentences the way a native writer would.
- **One language per piece.** Bilingual layouts are reserved for specific needs, with separate blocks of equal weight.
- **Names never change.** Brand and product names, model names and labels quoted from our software stay exactly as they are.
- **Plan for longer text.** Running text in Spanish and Catalan is about 15 to 30% longer than in English. Short strings grow far more: a button or tab label can double or triple in length. Design buttons, tabs and figure labels with that room, or rewrite the label.
- **Mark the language.** Every page and every passage in another language carries its `lang` attribute (`en`, `es`, `ca`), so screen readers pronounce it and browsers hyphenate it correctly.

## Languages and scope

| Site | Languages |
| --- | --- |
| **AIS Vision Systems** | Spanish (default), Catalan, English, French, Portuguese |
| **Rosepetal** | English |
| **AIS Group** | English |

- **A page that has not been localised does not exist in that language.** Never publish a fallback, an «only available in Spanish» stub, a title in the source language or an `hreflang` link to a page that is not there. Show readers the pages that exist.
- **Legal and system text is copy.** Privacy and consent notices, cookie tables, form messages, error pages and emails are localised like any page, in the page’s language. Legal notices use the formal register (*usted*, *vós*) consistently; the company is written *AIS Vision Systems, S.L.*
- **Quoting another language.** A testimonial may stay in its original language only if it is marked with `lang` and followed by a translation.
- **French and Portuguese** follow the same principles, with a named native writer and reviewer for each. French puts a non-breaking space before `: ; ! ? %` and inside «  »; Portuguese (Portugal) follows the 1990 spelling agreement. Both use a decimal comma and a space for thousands.

Use a non-breaking space between a number and its unit or % sign, so they never split across lines. Write the degree sign `°`, never the ordinal `º`, and no space before `, . : ;` in any of the three languages. In files and code, dates are always ISO: `2026-10-14`.

Two of our English choices differ from GOV.UK on purpose. We use the 24-hour clock and put a space before units, as SI does, because our readers are engineers in many countries and our products print times and measurements that way.

## Marketing and product text

| Text | English | Spanish | Catalan |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Calls to action** (web, campaigns) | Imperative: *Book a demo* | Imperative, tú: *Solicita una demo* | Imperative, tu: *Demana una demostració* |
| **Commands** (buttons, menus in the product) | Imperative: *Save*, *Export* | Infinitive: *Guardar*, *Exportar* | Imperative, tu: *Desa*, *Exporta* |
| **Error messages** | What happened, then what to do: *The file could not be saved. Check the disk space.* | *No se puede guardar el archivo. Comprueba el espacio en disco.* | *S’ha produït un error en desar el fitxer. Comprova l’espai al disc.* |

Never translate *Sorry* or *Please* into an error message, and drop exclamation marks unless data is at risk. Keep keyboard shortcuts as written in the software: `Ctrl+S`.

## Spanish

- Use **tú** with imperative, direct verbs in marketing: *Solicita una demo*, *Consulta la ficha*. A company reading the page is *tú*, never *vosotros*: write *tu empresa… orientarte… tu línea*, not *tu empresa… orientaros… vuestra línea*.
- Write **Spain Spanish**: *costes*, *ordenador*, *supervisión*, not *costos*, *computadora*, *monitoreo*.
- Write **IA**, never *AI*. Use *aprendizaje profundo* in running text; *deep learning* may follow in italics in brackets the first time, for engineers.
- The generic masculine is correct. Prefer collective or impersonal nouns where it reads naturally: *el equipo de calidad*, *la plantilla*. Never use slashes, `@`, `-x` or `-e` as gender marks.
- Avoid anglicisms when a precise Spanish term exists: *conjunto de datos* rather than *dataset* in running text, *entrenamiento* for *training*. An English term kept in running text goes in italics; a label on screen stays as it is.
- Write acronyms without plurals or points: *los PLC*, *las API*.

## Catalan

- Use **tu**: *Demana una demostració*, *Consulta la fitxa*. A company reading the page is *tu*: write *la teva empresa… orientar-te… la teva línia*, not *orientar-vos… la vostra línia*. In formal documents use **vós** (*us podem enviar*), never a mix of forms.
- Write **IA**, never *AI*, and *aprenentatge profund* in running text.
- Avoid common calques from Spanish: *en base a* (write *a partir de*, *segons*), *degut a* (*a causa de*), *a nivell de* (*pel que fa a*), *llevar a terme* (*dur a terme*), and *realitzar* where *fer* will do.
- Keep the **punt volat**: *col·laboració*, *instal·lació*, *intel·ligència*. Never replace it with a full stop or hyphen. At a line break, split the two l’s with a hyphen: *col-* / *laboració*.
- Use the typographic apostrophe (’) and elide by pronunciation: *l’Excel*, *l’HTML*, *la GPL*.
- Prefer *tothom*, collective nouns (*la plantilla*, *el professorat*) and phrases with *persones* to doubled forms. Slashes (*director/a*) only in forms and tables.
- Follow the IEC for spelling and TERMCAT for terms: *sol·licitud*, *conjunt de dades*, *entrenament del model*, *visió artificial*, *informàtica al núvol*.

## English

- Use **British spelling** with *-ise*: *colour*, *optimise*, *centre*, *modelling*. LanguageTool accepts *-ize* too, so the brand checks flag it.
- Keep sentences short and verbs active: *Book a demo*, *Train in the cloud*. Use full verb forms, including *cannot*.
- Spell out an acronym on first use unless the audience uses it daily (PLC, GPU, API). No full stops in acronyms.
- Write **machine vision**, never *artificial vision*. Rosepetal may say *computer vision* to developers.
- Dates in prose read *6 to 8 October 2026*, never *October 6th–8th, 2026*. Pages carry `lang="en"`.

## Checks before publishing

Every text passes all of these for its language. A flag that remains is either fixed or recorded as a false positive with a reason.

| Check | What it catches | Tool |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Grammar and style** | Agreement, typos, missing ¿ ¡, wrong apostrophes, common anglicisms | [LanguageTool](https://languagetool.org) (`en-GB`, `es`, `ca-ES`); for Catalan the [Softcatalà corrector](https://www.softcatala.org/corrector/) uses the same engine |
| **Spelling** | Misspellings, with a shared list of brand and product names | Hunspell dictionaries: `en_GB`, `es_ES`, `ca` (Softcatalà) |
| **Brand conventions** | `%` without a space in Spanish and Catalan, a point in thousands, *l.l* for *l·l*, straight apostrophes, *vostè*, *-ize*, contracted negatives, listed anglicisms | The brand language check (`language-check.mjs` in the brand assets) |
| **Build** | Non-breaking spaces before units and %, «» in Markdown in every language (the default typographic quotes are “ ”) | A step in each site’s build, so writers do not have to type them |
| **Wrong language** | A passage in another language, a template string left in the source language | A language detector per page, and the review |
| **Terminology** | A term that differs from the glossary | The glossary above; TERMCAT and Optimot for new terms |
| **Layout** | Headlines, buttons and labels that break badly or overflow | Every language at desktop and phone width |

### Review

1. **Approve the source first.** Never localise copy that is still changing.
2. **Glossary first.** A new term gets looked up (TERMCAT for Catalan, FundéuRAE and the RAE for Spanish) and added to the glossary with its source and date.
3. **Native writing.** A native writer localises the piece. Machine translation may produce a first draft only, labelled as such.
4. **Native review.** A different native reviewer checks meaning against the source, the glossary, the tone and the conventions on this page.
5. **Automated checks.** The checks above, until nothing is left unexplained.
6. **Layout check.** Every language at desktop and phone width.

Corrections that recur become glossary entries or new brand checks, so the same mistake is caught automatically next time.
