# Formats

Each format: the rule, then a rewrite. The "after" lines come from approved AIS pieces or follow them; figures in square brackets stand for a sourced value and never ship as written.

## Headline

One idea, 6–12 words, sentence case, from a formula on [Voice](../../communications/references/voice.md#headline-formulas). Nothing above or beside it. No pair of slogans joined by a colon, no exclamation mark, no Title Case.

| Before | After |
| --- | --- |
| Visión artificial de vanguardia: calidad sin límites | Un cuerpo extraño sobre el film |
| Unlock flawless quality with AI-powered inspection | A foreign body on the lidding film |
| Inspecciona. Rechaza. Registra. | Control del tapón y del nivel de llenado |

## Post caption

Two to four sentences: what the machine checks, on what, what the picture shows; then one approved call to action. Say when a picture is illustrative. No hashtag walls, no emoji, no question you answer yourself.

- ES: *AIS KARIKÓ inspecciona las bandejas termoselladas por ambas caras. En esta bandeja de muestra, un fragmento de plástico azul ha quedado sobre el film, junto a la etiqueta. Es un ejemplo ilustrativo, no el resultado de una prueba de máquina. Envíanos muestras*
- EN: *AIS KARIKÓ inspects heat-sealed trays from both sides. On this sample tray, a fragment of blue plastic has landed on the lidding film, next to the label. It is an illustrative example, not the result of a machine trial. Send us samples*

Not: *En el mundo actual, la seguridad alimentaria es más importante que nunca. ¿Sabías que…? Descubre cómo AIS KARIKÓ revoluciona tu línea.*

## Brochure paragraph

The lead is one or two sentences that say what the machine does and on what. Each block below argues one thing in 40–70 words. Short section labels are fine in print; taglines and closing slogans are not.

- *Comprueba que el tapón está presente, íntegro y bien colocado, controla el precinto y el nivel, y lee el marcaje del envase. En vidrio y PET, desde 0,33 L hasta garrafas de varios litros.* (AIS Hopper brochure)
- EN: *It checks that the cap is present, intact and seated, controls the seal and the fill level, and reads the container's marking. On glass and PET, from 0.33 L bottles to multi-litre jugs.*

Not: *Una solución integral, flexible y escalable que no solo mejora la calidad, sino que también impulsa la eficiencia de tu planta.*

## Deck slide

One message per slide, about 40 words at most on the slide (more goes in the speaker notes). The title states the conclusion, not the topic: *Del cierre al nivel de llenado*, not *Beneficios*. No eyebrow, no subtitle; a lead only when the title cannot carry the point alone. Bullets are parallel fragments without full stops, three to five of them, each naming something checkable.

## Proposal section

A proposal is read by a buyer who signs it. Write scope, not promises:

1. What is included (machines, modules, software, installation, training), with model names exactly.
2. What is not included and what the customer provides (samples, line access, utilities).
3. Conditions and figures with their basis: *detection measured on [n] customer samples at [speed]*, never *guaranteed detection*.
4. Lead time, warranty and service, as in the commercial terms.

No openers, no adjectives about AIS, no summary at the end. Quotes and prices are internal: never in public pieces.

## Case study

Three parts, each a short block:

1. **Problem:** the line, the product, the defect and what it cost, in the customer's terms.
2. **Solution:** the machine and modules installed, what the model does and what a person reviews.
3. **Result:** the metric, baseline, new value, line conditions and period, labelled measured.

Publish only with the customer's written approval of the name, the quote and the figures; without it, describe the customer precisely (*a cava producer in Penedès, four lines*). A quote is the customer's exact words with name and role, never polished.
