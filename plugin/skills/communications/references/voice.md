# Voice

Write plainly, support claims with evidence and name products consistently.

## Traits

We write like the engineers who install and support the machine. Name the defect, the line and the result; show the evidence; offer one clear next step.

| Trait | Do | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Specific** | Name the product, the defect, the container and the line condition: *cracked neck finishes on returnable glass at line speed*. | «Any defect», «all products», «maximum precision». |
| **Measured** | Use the metric the plant already tracks (false rejects, detection rate, OEE, changeover time, bottles per hour), with its conditions. | Metrics of our own invention, multipliers without a baseline, dropping «up to» from a quoted figure. |
| **Plain** | Short sentences, active verbs, one idea per sentence. Spell out an acronym once unless the reader uses it daily. | Abstract taglines that fit any company, stacked adjectives, Title Case headlines. |
| **Honest about AI** | Say what the model does, what data it needs, who reviews the result and what still uses rule-based tools. | «Revolutionary AI», «autonomous», «self-learning», AI as the only argument. |
| **Close** | Write as the team that installs and supports the machine: *we*, *you*, the people on the line. | Corporate distance («AIS Group is a leading provider of…») or false familiarity. |
| **Useful** | End with one next step that names its destination: *Request a feasibility study*, *Download the datasheet (PDF, 2 MB)*. | «Learn more», «Discover more», several actions of equal weight. |

## Structure

Lead in this order: **need, solution, evidence, next step**.

| Element | Question it answers | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Headline** | Why should I care? | One idea, 6–12 words, sentence case. |
| **Intro** | What does this piece offer? | One or two sentences of context. |
| **Body** | How does it work, what does it bring? | One argument per block. |
| **Evidence** | What supports it? | A real image of the defect, a figure with its conditions, a diagram or a case. |
| **Action** | What do I do next? | One call to action from the approved set. |

### Headline formulas

| Formula | Example | Use for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **[Verb] [defect] on [object], [condition]** | *Detect cracked neck finishes on returnable glass, at full line speed* | AIS Vision Systems product pages |
| **[Result] without [usual cost]** | *Fewer false rejects without slowing the line* | Benefit sections |
| **[Metric]: [figure] at [customer]** | *False rejects down from 1.8% to 0.4% at [customer]* | Case studies, once the figure is approved |
| **From [input] to [output]** | *From images to a model running on your line* | Rosepetal |
| **[Task] for [sector]** | *Label inspection for wine and spirits* | Sector pages |
| **The question the reader already asks** | *Is your inspector rejecting good bottles?* | Campaigns and articles |

No three-word structures (*Inspect. Reject. Record.*): they are taglines. Spanish and Catalan headlines are written from the idea, not from the English words.

## Claims and evidence

Write every claim as: *[result] in [metric], from [baseline] to [new value], on [object and line conditions], over [period], at [customer or test]; measured, estimated, target or illustrative.* A lab measurement is never a production guarantee.

- **One figure, one source.** Public figures (customers, installations, units inspected, founding year, years of experience) live in one figures register per brand, with source and date. Copy quotes the register, so the same fact never appears with two values on two sites.
- **Name a customer only with written permission.** Otherwise describe them precisely: *a cava producer in Penedès, four lines*.
- **Show the defect.** Real images of the defect classes are our strongest evidence.
- **Say what AI does not do.** *The model flags; the operator confirms new defect types* reassures food and pharma readers.
- **Keep the qualifiers.** A figure goes in the body with its conditions before it goes in a headline, and the headline never drops «up to».
- **No implied certifications.** Captions and alt text never suggest a certification or accreditation unless they name the certificate.
- **Never invent** testimonials, customers, certifications or partner logos.

| Instead of | Write |
| --- | --- |
| «Zero defects», «100% detection», «guarantees» | The detection rate under stated conditions. (*100% inspection*, meaning every unit passes the inspection point, is a correct term when true.) |
| «Reduced false rejects by 70×» | The baseline, the new value, the line and the period |
| «Up to 30% savings» | The indicator, the baseline, the conditions and the evidence |
| «Cutting-edge AI», «AI-powered» as the argument | The task the model performs and the benefit shown |
| «Fully autonomous», «self-learning» | What the system automates and what a person reviews |
| «The best», «world-leading», «the most advanced on the market», «unmatched» | A verifiable fact, or nothing |
| «Seamless», «effortless» | The steps and the time they take |
| «Unlock», «leverage», «empower» | A plain verb: *cut*, *find*, *check* |
| «An integral solution» | The thing itself: *inspector*, *software*, *module* |

## Names

Write brand and product names exactly as in the [names table](../../ais-brand-guidelines/references/brands.md) on Brand architecture, which also lists the forms never to use. These names go in the spelling dictionary of every language.

## Template text is copy too

Footers, cookie banners, consent text, form messages, error pages and emails follow the same voice, the same address form and the same language as the page. They are where large brands most often slip: an English button on a Spanish page, a formal *usted* in a *tú* site, a date in the wrong format.

## Writing that reads as ours

Generated text has habits readers spot at once. Leave them out, in every language.

- Short sentences, one idea each. Name the object, the figure and where the figure comes from.
- Plain verbs instead of hype: never *unlock*, *elevate*, *seamless*, *cutting-edge*, *revolutionise*, *game-changer*, *world-class*; *innovative*, *powerful* and *advanced* only with the fact that proves them.
- Start with the point and stop when it is made: no openers about the world (*In today’s fast-paced world*, *In the age of AI*) and no closing summaries (*In short*, *In conclusion*).
- No *not only… but also*, no *more than just*, no three adjectives to fill a line, no three-word slogans.
- No rhetorical questions in body copy. A headline may ask the question the reader already asks.
- No em dash as a habit: a comma, a colon or a full stop does the job.
- Headlines that say something end with a full stop, and a two-part headline takes one after each part: *Computer vision. Ready for production.* Names and labels never take one: page and section names, product and company names, card and chart titles, table headers, navigation and footer headings. A question ends with «?»; a heading never ends with a colon.
- No helper text on the piece: *click*, *learn more about*, *discover how*, *scroll*. The layout shows the way and the call to action names the destination.

The AIS Group Design plugin carries this list in its `communications` skill, and its text check runs it on every post, carousel, brochure, catalogue, datasheet, deck and stationery piece before export.

Never use a middle dot as a separator. Only Catalan l·l spelling and explanations of it are allowed. Separate elements or lines, use a comma or write «in». Do not substitute a bar, dash or slash.
