# Realism

The bar: nothing at the delivered size gives the picture away as computer-made. Every iteration, put a real photo of the same product type beside the render at the same scale, list the differences and fix the worst three. Report only when nothing gives it away, with the side-by-side.

## Labels look like real packaging

- **AIS demo products** carry the AIS Vision Systems logo, a real product name and a design that fits the category: warm, appetising palettes for food, serif and foil for wine. Never a real brand or an implied certification. Vary pack designs between categories so products don't look alike.
- **Food packs carry a real food photograph**, as real packs do, from a public-domain, CC0 or CC BY source, with the author, licence and address in the label script.
- **Real print content**: dense legal text at real sizes, a valid in-store EAN-13 (prefix 200 to 299), the lot and date in the coder's 5 × 7 dot matrix, and «Muestra de demostración. No apta para la venta». No lorem ipsum, fake glyphs or floating marks.
- **A customer's product** is built from the customer's real capture: crop each label where it faces its camera square on, remove the capture's lighting, and correct the colour against white paper in the shot. The label paper keeps the real paper's colour (warm ivory on many wine labels), never pure white.
- **Printed codes are print-crisp at 100 %**: rebuild an EAN-13 as vectors from the digits under the real bars, and a QR code from its real modules thresholded onto its grid. Never encode new content.
- **The print has a substrate**: paper fibre or film, a slight varnish sheen, ink that never reaches pure white or black (albedo 0.05 to 0.86).

## Materials and liquids

- Glass has a faint green-blue edge tint, thick dark edges at the silhouette, a mould seam, faint fingerprints or dust in the roughness, and the correct index of refraction.
- Liquids take their colour from volume absorption and scattering, not from a base colour: orange juice turbid with pulp, red wine near-black in the body and ruby at a thin edge, sparkling wine pale straw with a few bubbles at the wall, saline water-clear with a meniscus.
- Metals have brushed or rolled anisotropy and varied roughness; plastics a satin moulded texture; board a scanned paper texture; food subsurface scattering.
- Real closures: cap ribs, tamper rings, crimp pleats, foil folds.
- Nothing is perfectly clean or regular: small variation between units in turn, height, crimp and label placement.

## Light and camera

- A packshot setup: a key softbox from the upper left, two strip lights for edge highlights on glass, a fill card and black flags. No flat ambient light.
- Contact shadows: the product sits on the surface.
- An 85 to 100 mm lens, a realistic aperture, focus on the label; slight depth of field on far units.
- No clipped whites (peaks at about 240 of 255); a little grain.

## One set, one photographer

Every picture of a set looks shot by one photographer in one studio:

- the same seamless `#F1F5F9` ground for product shots, the same light rig from the same side, the same materials;
- the same camera for each role:
  - **cover lineup**: isolated products seen slightly above label height, a real count and arrangement, the defect off-centre (never three in a row with the middle one wrong);
  - **defect pair**: good and defective framed identically, upright products side by side;
  - **code close-up**: straight down, dome light;
  - **still life**: three-quarter, room on the right for the headline.

Before showing a set, put its cover and one pair next to the last accepted set at feed size. If it stands out in light, colour, framing or finish, fix it.

## The machine and its line

- A bright, clean hall: near-white floor with yellow walkway lines, soft daylight; no dark industrial mood, glow, beams or text in the picture.
- The stainless machine on a straight conveyor. Belts and chains are brown, grey or blue as real ones are; side guides are stainless or black, never coloured.
- Credible line parts where real: infeed, the photocell (a physical sensor, no visible beam), the reject unit and its lane, the Panel PC with its tower light.
- Products run at the pitch and speed the piece claims, never faster, and each defective product carries only its own defect.
- Physics is right before anything moves: contact shadows, scale against the machine, reflections of the hood and rails in glass and metal, nothing floating or passing through anything.
- Long wides don't repeat one leg and foot pattern.

## The review

Use `render-review`. Deliver one still per shot, a contact sheet, and a 100 % crop of each label and code next to its real source. Zoom on every mark; check each label is on the right object and each defect reads in one second at the delivered size.
